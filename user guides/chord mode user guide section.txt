CHORD MODE

Chord Mode turns the instrument into a comping instrument. The octave rollers
stop selecting octaves and instead select chord quality; your fingering supplies
the chord root. Every note you blow produces a voiced jazz chord rather than a
single pitch, voice led against the chord before it.

Because the register comes from the voicing algorithm rather than from your
fingering, only the pitch class of the fingered note matters. Fingering a C
anywhere on the horn gives a chord rooted on C.

This is a comping mode, not a lead mode. The voicings deliberately stay low and
narrow so they sit under whoever is soloing.


TURNING IT ON

Select CHD in POLY PLAY - POLY MODE, then arm it the same way as the other poly
modes: hold the Pinky Key (NuEVI) or MOD key (NuRAD) and touch the Special Key.
Touch Pinky + Special Key again to turn it off.

While Chord Mode is armed the octave rollers no longer change octave. Disarming
it returns the rollers to normal octave duty immediately.


SIMPLE CHORD MODE (CHS)

Select CHS instead of CHD for plain chords. Arming, rollers and roller slots
are exactly the same, but every chord is just the four chord tones - root, 3rd,
5th, 7th - in close position (all within one octave). No extensions, no
rootless voicings, no randomness.

The first chord after power-on is in root position, root between C3 and B3.
After that each chord takes whichever inversion of those four notes moves the
voices the least from the chord before - a ii-V-I moves about three semitones
in total per change. Every chord stays between Bb2 and C5, so the voicing can
never wander off. CHORD BASS, CHORD LIL, CHORD TEMP and CHORD TOP have no
effect in CHS.

  dim7  R b3 b5 bb7     m7b5  R b3 b5 b7     min7  R b3 5 b7
  dom7  R 3 5 b7        maj7  R 3 5 7        sus7  R 4 5 b7


VOICE LEADING ACROSS BREATHS

Both CHD and CHS lead each chord from the previous one even when you tongue
or take a breath between them, not only when you change fingering while
blowing. The instrument remembers the last chord it played until you power
off. For the strictly smoothest choice every time in CHD, set CHORD TEMP to 0.


TELLING THE SYNTH

Arming and disarming Chord Mode (CHD or CHS) sends CC86 = 127 / CC86 = 0, and
CC86 = 127 is repeated every 2 seconds while armed and not playing. Most synths
ignore it (CC86 is undefined in the MIDI spec). The RaspiSynth uses it to
switch the current patch to polyphonic and turn chords down to single-note
level while Chord Mode is armed, and back to the patch's own mono settings when
it is disarmed. For any other synth, use a polyphonic patch: a mono patch plays
only one note of each chord.


THE ROLLERS

Each roller slot is one chord quality:

  Roller 1  dim7
  Roller 2  m7b5
  Roller 3  min7
  Roller 4  dom7
  Roller 5  maj7
  Roller 6  sus7

The NuRAD has six roller pads, so slot 6 is a pad of its own. The NuEVI has
five, and slot 6 is the R5 + R3 combination - touch both together.

The order is chosen so that neighbouring slots are neighbours in real
progressions, not in abstract theory. A major ii-V-I is simply rollers 3, 4, 5
in sequence. The axis also runs dark to bright, so roller position has an
intuitive meaning rather than being an arbitrary lookup.

Rest your thumb on the min7/dom7 boundary - that is where most of the playing is.

A minor ii-V is awkward: m7b5 (2) to dom7 (4) to min7 (3) is a two step jump and
then a reversal. This is unavoidable, because minor ii-V wants m7b5 next to dom7
while major ii-V wants min7 next to dom7, and min7 sits between them on any
brightness axis. Major ii-V is far more common, so it wins.

Lifting your thumb off the rollers entirely does not change the chord. The last
quality you selected is held, in every roller mode.

Changing quality while a note is still sounding does not retrigger the chord.
Only the voices that actually move are re-struck; everything else keeps its
envelope. Fingering a new root does retrigger the whole chord.


POLY PLAY - CHORD BASS

WITH - somebody else is playing bass. The root is dropped entirely and you play
rootless four note upper structures in the Bill Evans tradition, kept above C3 so
they stay out of the bass player's register. This is the default.

SOLO - you cover root duty yourself. A root is added in the C2 to B2 window with
the same upper structures above it, exactly the way a pianist plays it with the
left hand. Three note reduced upper forms also become available in this mode, so
the chord stays at four or five notes rather than growing to six.


POLY PLAY - CHORD LIL

Shifts the low interval limit table, which is what stops close intervals turning
to mud in the low register. Range is -12 to +6 semitones, 0 is the standard
orchestral table.

Negative allows lower and closer voicings. Positive is more conservative. The
right value depends on how much low harmonic content your synth patch carries,
so tune this once by ear against the patch you actually play - a thin patch can
take a negative value, a rich sawtooth pad usually wants 0 or above.

At the extreme positive end a small number of chords fall back to a plain shell
voicing (root and guide tones). That is by design and always sounds clean, just
plainer.


POLY PLAY - CHORD TEMP

How much the voicing selection varies between repeats of the same chord. Stored
in tenths, so 20 is 2.0, the default.

In practice this dial does very little, because after filtering there are only
ever two to eight usable voicings for any given chord. That is intentional: for
a sustaining instrument, predictable is better than varied. Set it to 0 for
completely deterministic voicing. If you want more variety, this is not the lever
- the constraint system is already doing that job.


POLY PLAY - CHORD TOP

Drops the top voice band, in semitones, 0 to 5. 0 puts the top voice between Bb3
and B4.

The top voice is the one the audience hears as the tune, so it is deliberately
kept low and stable. If the soloist is playing low - bass clarinet, baritone,
guitar in low position - raise this to move the whole comp down out of their way
rather than letting it float above them. At the maximum setting a few chords fall
back to shell voicings, as above.


NOTES

Extensions are fixed per quality and there are no altered dominants. Whether a
G7 wants a b9 or a natural 9 depends almost entirely on the chord that follows
it, which the instrument cannot know at the moment you blow. A wrong guess there
is a real clash against the soloist, not a mild blemish, so the line is drawn at
alterations. Natural extensions do not have that problem - a 9 on a min7 is a 9
regardless of what follows - so those are always present:

  maj7   root 3 5 7 9
  dom7   root 3 5 b7 9 13
  min7   root b3 5 b7 9 11
  m7b5   root b3 b5 b7 11
  sus7   root 4 5 b7 9
  dim7   root b3 b5 bb7

Velocity is scaled down as the chord gets denser, so a five note voicing does not
arrive five times louder than a single line. If your synth ignores velocity and
runs its amplitude straight from breath, apply the same scaling on the synth side
or chords will be much louder than single notes.

Chord Mode has no separate voice limit setting. HMZ LIMIT applies to the key
based harmonizers only, not here.
